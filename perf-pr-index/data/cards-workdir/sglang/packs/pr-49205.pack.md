## Motivation

Builds on #49003, which runs carv2 at TP16 across two MI455X hosts in DPX mode. A DPX host splits each SoC into two partitions. #49003 treats all 16 ranks as flat peers, so `2shot_push` sends almost the whole tensor over the fabric twice. But the two partitions of a SoC share its HBM, and traffic between them is cheaper than traffic between SoCs. On one host, TP2 with both ranks on one SoC vs on two SoCs (bf16, CUDA graph, us):

| Size | 1shot_push, same SoC | 1shot_push, two SoCs | 2shot_push, same SoC | 2shot_push, two SoCs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 KB | 2.3 | 5.2 | 3.1 | 7.5 |
| 4 MB | 13.7 | 25.9 | 18.4 | 18.0 |
| 8 MB | 24.8 | 44.6 | 19.5 | 28.7 |

This PR reduces each pair first, so only half the tensor crosses the fabric, and every SoC's fabric traffic drops by about half. At TP16 that makes carv2 17-31% faster than the flat `2shot_push` from 384 KB to 64 MB, and 7-15% faster with one rank arriving late. It only kicks in when every SoC has both of its partitions in the group. Other world sizes, SPX, CUDA and other ROCm GPUs keep today's behavior.

## Modifications

**Algorithm.** `hier_2shot_push` runs in three steps for N bytes:

1. Pair reduce-scatter. Partition p of each SoC ends up with the pair sum of half p.
2. `2shot_push` of half p across partition p of every SoC (8 ranks at TP16).
3. Pair all-gather.

**Kernel** (`custom_all_reduce.cuh`, ROCm only)

- `pair_push_kernel<T, kAllGather>` is one half of a 2-rank `2shot_push`: reduce-scatter or all-gather.
- It uses the same Lamport markers and per-block epochs as `2shot_push`. Each launch pushes to the peer and also polls and clears what the peer sent, so consecutive reduce-scatter and all-gather launches alternate epochs in step on both ranks. They can therefore share a one-chunk slot.
- The kernel and its dispatch are under `#ifdef USE_ROCM`. The CUDA build is unchanged and `communicator.cuh` is untouched.

**Topology** (`gfx1250_fabric.py`)

- `dpx_pairs` / `probe_dpx_pairs` group ranks by the UALink `accel_id` that every rank reads from sysfs. Ranks with the same ID are the two partitions of one SoC.
- The probe is collective. If any rank fails to read its ID, or the layout is anything other than exact pairs (SPX, CPX, a SoC with only one partition in the group), every rank gets None and the algo stays off.

**`CustomAllReduceV2`**

- The pair and cross push planes are views at the end of the existing symmetric allocation, each with its own epoch counter. No extra process group or rendezvous.
- A pair slot holds half a message and stays within `max_size`, like every other push slot. With the default 16 MB, `hier_2shot_push` covers up to 32 MB and `2shot_push` keeps 32-64 MB.
- When `hier_2shot_push` covers the whole `2shot_push` range (`SGLANG_CUSTOM_ALL_REDUCE_V2_MAX_SIZE_KB=32768`), `2shot_push` keeps only the sizes below it, and the flat push slot shrinks from 8 MB to 256 KB. Symmetric memory per rank at TP16:
  - #49003: 256 MB.
  - Default `max_size`: 352 MB.
  - 32 MB `max_size`: 264 MB.
- Communicators that size their own push plane or run push-only (fused qk-norm and other per-layer instances) get no hier planes.

**gfx1250 table** (`configs/custom_all_reduce_v2.py`)

- The TP16 row picks `hier_2shot_push` from 256 KB to 64 MB. `1shot_push` keeps up to 256 KB.
- A size that is not a multiple of 32 B can't be split into whole 16 B halves. It goes to `2shot_push` where that still covers it, and to RCCL otherwise.

**Known interaction.** The Muse Spark fused MoE finalize + all-reduce only fuses where the standalone all-reduce picks `2shot_push`. At TP16 over DPX it therefore runs unfused (separate finalize, then `hier_2shot_push`) inside the hier range. The result is correct; I could not measure that model at TP16.

## Speed Tests and Profiling

All-reduce latency, TP16 over two DPX hosts, bf16, us per all-reduce (max over ranks), median of 3 runs, CUDA graph (16 all-reduces per graph). RCCL 2.32.3 uses its fabric DDA path. Improvement = (2shot_push - hier) / 2shot_push.

**How the range was chosen.**
- **Lower bound.** At 128 KB `1shot_push` is clearly faster (13.2 vs 19.4 us). At 256 KB `hier_2shot_push` is ahead both back to back (20.0 vs 21.4) and with a late rank (37.1 vs 42.0). The table keeps `1shot_push` up to 256 KB and starts hier right above it.
- **Upper bound.** From 32 to 64 MB, `hier_2shot_push` beats `2shot_push` in every mode, so the row goes to 64 MB.

**Follow-up.** In the middle stage, `hier_2shot_push` calls the 8-rank `2shot_push` kernel. A separate PR from @wenkaidu speeds that kernel up for 8 ranks or fewer, and this path picks that up without changes. Measured on top of it, hier was 21-30% faster than flat `2shot_push` from 8 to 64 MB.
