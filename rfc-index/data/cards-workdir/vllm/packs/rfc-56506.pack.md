## 正文(PRD / 提案全文)

## Motivation

DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash leaves a lot of the device on the table on AMD MI355X (gfx950). Measured on an 8x MI355X node, TP4, MXFP4 MoE + DSpark MTP:

| concurrency | out tok/s | out tok/s per GPU | TTFT p50 | ITL p50 | E2EL p50 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 35.89 | 8.97 | 0.898 | 0.0256 | 15.03 |
| 2 | 49.10 | 12.27 | 0.940 | 0.0267 | 17.22 |
| 4 | 86.99 | 21.75 | 0.634 | 0.0271 | 5.55 |
| 8 | 164.69 | 41.17 | 0.609 | 0.0307 | 12.10 |
| 16 | 292.60 | 73.15 | 0.684 | 0.0332 | 14.61 |
| 32 | 469.41 | 117.35 | 0.825 | 0.0477 | 17.57 |

The shape of that table is the telling part. Going from concurrency 1 to 32 costs only **1.9x** in ITL (0.0256 -> 0.0477) while delivering **13x** more output per GPU, so the device is nowhere near saturated: there is a large fixed cost per decode step that concurrency merely amortises. A captured decode trace says what that cost is. At a single-stream ITL of 25.6 ms, a decode step issues **14,020 kernel launches**, and 85% of each layer's 350 launches are attributable to the mHC block - bookkeeping on a 4x4 matrix.

| launches/layer | share | source |
|---:|---:|---|
| 162 | 48.6% | mHC sinkhorn loop |
| 122 | 36.6% | mHC pre/post mix + eager RMSNorm |
| 24 | 7.2% | MoE / FFN (already fused) |
| 12 | 3.6% | dtype casts/copies |
| 6 | 1.8% | attention + fused norm/quant |
| 4 | 1.2% | TP collective |
| 3 | 0.9% | other |

> **Trace methodology, since it affects what the numbers can support.** The trace had to be captured with `--enforce-eager`: with CUDA graphs on, roctracer's HSA queue interposition deadlocks against hipGraph replay on this ROCm build (the first step after `/start_profile` never completes and the workers spin in `queue_interposition.cpp` waiting on an HSA signal). So **only the launch counts and kernel composition should be read as load-bearing** - those are graph-invariant, since a graph replays the kernel sequence it captured rather than merging kernels. All wall-clock, occupancy and idle-gap figures from that trace are inflated by eager dispatch plus profiler overhead and are deliberately not used for any claim here. The throughput and ITL numbers above are from normal graph-enabled serving runs, not from the traced run.

This is purely a performance question - accuracy is already where it should be. Recording it here as the standard for the work below, since every item in this RFC is a performance change that has to hold accuracy. gsm8k 5-shot via lm-eval, same node and configuration:

| | strict-match | flexible-extract |
|---|---:|---:|
| baseline | 0.9704 ± 0.0047 | 0.9697 ± 0.0047 |
| with #56503 | 0.9727 ± 0.0045 | 0.9719 ± 0.0045 |
| with #56503 + #56513 | 0.9735 ± 0.0044 | 0.9727 ± 0.0045 |

Harness gate is 0.91. Anything landing against this RFC should be checked against the numbers above rather than just the gate, since a change can pass 0.91 while still having broken something.

What follows is grouped by optimization direction. Each item is verified against the code rather than assumed, and several turn out to be different from what they look like from the outside.

## 1. mHC seam fusion

The largest single direction, since 85% of per-layer launches live here.

### 1.1 mHC pre -> AITER

`vllm/models/deepseek_v4_1/amd/model.py` imports `mhc_pre_delayed_torch` / `mhc_post_torch` directly, so every sublayer seam spends ~141 launches Sinkhorn-normalizing a 4x4 matrix. V4 already dispatches through `MHCPreOp` / `MHCPostOp` and reaches AITER; V4.1 was never migrated, because it collapses each sublayer's input with the *previous* sublayer's pre-mix and AITER's `mhc_pre` neither returns its pre-mix nor accepts an incoming one.

Merged as #56503: 141 -> 4 launches per seam, worst relative error 1.2e-5, gsm8k unchanged.

At concurrency 1 that moved ITL p50 from 25.58 ms to 6.89 ms. Read that as directional only, not as a 3.7x per-token speedup: the agentic trace is time-driven, so the faster run sampled a different and longer segment (mean input 168,856 -> 206,458 tokens, mean output 954 -> 1,683). The confound runs against the result, but a clean figure needs a fixed-request-count replay. Details in the PR.

### 1.2 Fused mHC post+pre for V4.1

`deepseek_v4/amd/model.py` has 4 references to `MHCFusedPostPreOp` / `mhc_fused_post_pre`; `deepseek_v4_1/amd/model.py` has **zero**. `mhc_fused_post_pre_gemm_sqrsum` applies the post step and projects the resulting residual in one kernel, and has gfx950-specific tuning (`_mhc_fused_config_gfx950_256`). It needs the same `pre_mix` plumbing #56503 added, so it is a direct follow-on rather than separate work.

Sizing this honestly: because the delayed formulation still needs the pre-mix epilogue and the delayed collapse, folding in the post takes the seam from 6 launches to 5, not to 2. Measured on gfx950, all-reduce excluded: 5 -> 4.

Above 1024 tokens AITER's own heuristic prefers separate kernels, so this is a decode-path win by construction. The name misleads here: `mhc_fused_post_pre_large_m` is *not* a fused kernel, it is plain `mhc_post` + `mhc_pre` with a non-temporal store hint (`store_nt=0` above `8 * num_cu`) and `large_m_splitk=True`, neither of which the fused kernel can express. Once the residual stops fitting in cache there is nothing left for fusion to save.

Addressed by #56513 (draft). Measured at 1.28% lower mean ITL at concurrency 1, 95% CI [0.07%, 2.48%], as a two-way crossover over both halves of the node with a fixed 24-request replay -- the two GPU sets here differ by 0.96%, so a single-orientation A/B cannot resolve an effect this size. One loose end that change does not close: its fallback is the plain post plus delayed pre, so it asks for neither large-m tuning. Inert between 1024 and 2048 tokens, but a real miss at prefill, which reaches 16384 tokens here. Needs a `store_nt` argument on the shared `mhc_post` wrapper that V4 also uses.

Note `_AITER_MHC_FUSED_RMSNORM_SIZES = {1280, 2560, 4096, 7168}` excludes V4.1-Flash's `hidden_size=5120`, so the fused-RMSNorm variant does not apply here; extending that set, or the kernel's supported sizes, is a separate question.

**Parallel ROCm work on the same seam.** #56342 reaches 1.1/1.2 by a different route: instead of dispatching the AMD path through AITER, it fixes the TileLang `mhc_pre_big_fuse*` kernels on 64-wide wavefronts and moves V4.1's AMD path onto the fused TileLang kernels. Its own measurement is that the switch without the wavefront fix is a 91.1% -> 23.7% gsm8k regression, so both halves land together. That makes it and #56503/#56513 two answers to the same question -- which backend owns the V4.1 AMD mHC seam -- and they should be reconciled before either goes further.

### 1.3 Fused all-reduce + mHC post

Worth being explicit about why this, and not all-reduce + RMSNorm, which is the more obvious proposal. Three reasons, in increasing order of decisiveness:

1. **The ROCm AR+RMSNorm pass exists and auto-enables.** `RocmAiterAllReduceFusionPass` is selected when `fuse_allreduce_rms` is set and AITER is enabled (`vllm/compilation/passes/pass_manager.py:176-180`), and `enable_allreduce_rms_fusion` returns true on ROCm for TP>1 (`vllm/config/vllm.py:171-185`). Our server duly logs `Enabled custom fusions: ..., allreduce_rms, ...`.
2. **But it is an Inductor FX pattern matcher, and this model is invisible to it.** There is no `@support_torch_compile` anywhere under `vllm/models/deepseek_v4_1/` (verified: zero matches), so the pass never sees the model graph. "Enabled" in that log is misleading.
3. **And the pattern is not in the model anyway.** V4.1's decoder has no `all_reduce -> RMSNorm` seam. The TP all-reduce lives inside `_o_proj` / `_bpre_attn_gemm(..., reduce_tp=True)`, and what follows it is the **mHC post block**. Contrast `deepseek_v32/amd/model.py`, which does call `fused_allreduce_rms_norm` explicitly.

The fusion that does apply is already written and unused: `aiter/ops/custom_all_reduce.py` defines `fused_allreduce_mhc_post_only`, `_one_stage` and `_split` plus `launch_*` wrappers, backed by the prebuilt `aiter/jit/module_fused_ar_mhc.so`. vLLM has **zero** references to any of them. At 4 collective launches per layer, wiring this removes both the collective and the post kernel from each seam.

Proposal: add `rocm_aiter_ops.fused_allreduce_mhc_post` and call it from the V4.1 AMD seam. Separately, `docs/design/fusions.md:101-107` still documents AR+RMSNorm as NVIDIA Hopper/Blackwell + FlashInfer only and should be corrected now that the AITER path exists.

**Correction: 1.2 and 1.3 are alternatives, not additive.** Both consume the same post step -- 1.2 folds it forward into the pre projection, 1.3 folds it backward into the collective -- so whichever lands, the other has nothing left to fuse. Each is worth one launch out of six. AITER ships only `fused_allreduce_mhc_post_{only,one_stage,split}`; there is no AR+post+pre kernel that would get both. 1.3 also needs the un-reduced attention output, which today is consumed inside `_o_proj`, so it means plumbing a deferred-all-reduce contract through `DeepseekV4Attention.forward` for both the attention and MoE seams.

Given equal payoff, I am pursuing 1.2 (self-contained) and leaving 1.3 unclaimed. It becomes clearly worthwhile if someone adds a three-way AR+post+pre kernel, which would take the seam to 4.

## 2. Multi-stream overlap

The interesting direction, because the code is already written and currently does nothing on the decode path.

### 2.1 Existing overlap is disabled by breakable-cudagraph capture

V4.1 attention **already** overlaps three aux streams (`vllm/models/deepseek_v4_1/attention.py`): input projections via `execute_in_parallel` (`793-801`), Q/SWA-cache-insert against the compressor forward (`688-697`), and indexer prep against `compressor.insert_cache` (`713-720`). V4.1 AMD even allocates them unconditionally - `aux_stream_list = [torch.cuda.Stream() for _ in range(3)]` (`amd/model.py:376-380`) - with no `is_rocm()` gate, unlike V4 AMD which disables them outright for hang reasons (`deepseek_v4/amd/model.py:964-968`).

But both overlap helpers drop to sequential execution during breakable CUDA graph capture:

```python
# vllm/utils/multi_stream_utils.py
if aux_stream is not None:
    from vllm.compilation.breakable_cudagraph import BreakableCUDAGraphCapture
    if BreakableCUDAGraphCapture.is_active():
        aux_stream = None
```

Our runs log `Breakable CUDA graph enabled`. Because the decode graph is **captured** serially, every replay is serial too, so on the decode path all of this overlap is dead. Prefill, uncaptured, still gets it.

The ask is therefore to make the existing overlap survive capture - record the aux streams into the graph, or exempt these regions - and then confirm gfx950 does not reproduce the hangs that caused V4 AMD to disable aux streams. The stale comments at `attention.py:429-430` ("Will be None on ROCm for now") and `690` ("sequential on ROCm") describe V4, not V4.1, and should be fixed alongside.

One genuine scheduling gap remains beyond that: `indexer_op` -> `forward_mqa` is fully serialized (`attention.py:817-829`). The top-k indices are a hard dependency, but MLA prep could plausibly be hoisted to overlap the indexer.

On terminology: there is no `CSA2` in the tree. CSA/HCA refers to the compressor path, and V4.1's `DeepseekCompressor` supports `compress_ratio` 1 and 2 only (ratio-4/128 is v4.0 CuTe-DSL and explicitly not wired).

### 2.2 DeepSeek MoE gets neither stream overlap nor kernel fusion

The generic `MoERunner` supports shared-expert overlap on the aux stream (`SharedExpertsOrder.MULTI_STREAM_OVERLAPPED`, gated `is_cuda_alike()` plus a 256-token threshold), and Inkling's AMD MoE uses `maybe_execute_in_parallel` for exactly this. DeepSeek AMD MoE uses neither, because it is meant to get shared-expert fusion at the kernel level via `fuse_heterogeneous_shared_expert`.

That fusion requires **all** of gfx950, `tensor_parallel_size == 8`, and `moe_backend == "aiter"` (`deepseek_v4/amd/model.py:210-231`). At TP4 with `aiter_triton_mxfp4_bf16` we satisfy only the first, so the shared expert is simply serialized against the routed experts. Either relax the fusion gate or enable the aux-stream path for DeepSeek on ROCm. Note this interacts with 2.1: while overlap is serialized during capture, the stream option buys nothing.

**Correction: relaxing the TP8 gate is not sufficient.** Reading the gate and AITER together, `fuse_heterogeneous_shared_expert` is pinned to DeepSeek-V4-Pro's geometry, not merely to TP8:

- `_heterogeneous_shared_expert_enabled` also requires `hidden_size == 7168` and `moe_intermediate_size == 3072`. V4.1-Flash is 5120 / 2304.
- It requires the shared expert as block-128 FP8 (`weight_block_size == [128, 128]`). V4.1-Flash ships it as FP8 E4M3 with **32x32** E8M0 scales -- `layers.N.ffn.shared_experts.w1.weight` is `[2304, 5120]` with `.scale` `[72, 160]` -- and `_prepare_native_fp8_shared_expert` validates the `(2*inter/128, hidden/128)` scale shape before expanding it to FHMoE's 1x32 layout, so it raises on ours rather than falling back.
- AITER's tuned config has no row for us. `aiter/configs/tuned_fhmoe.csv` is 12 rows, every one `model_dim=7168, inter_dim=384, expert=385, topk=7` -- V4-Pro at TP8, since 3072/8 = 384, which is what the `i384` in `supports_dsv4_i384_fhmoe()` names. That helper probes only that tuple.

The homogeneous alternative is closed too: `VLLM_ROCM_USE_AITER_FUSION_SHARED_EXPERTS` runs `is_shared_expert_quant_fse_compatible`, which requires a Quark MXFP4 OCP export and returns *"DeepSeek-v4 FSE is only implemented/tested with Quark MXFP4"* for this DeepSeek-native `quant_method: fp8` / `expert_dtype: fp4` checkpoint. The heterogeneous kernel exists precisely because FP8-shared-plus-FP4-routed cannot fuse homogeneously, which is our case, just locked to the wrong geometry.

Traces agree with the reading: no `flydsl_*` kernel appears anywhere in a decode step, AITER's dispatch reports `expert=384, topk=6` rather than 385/7, and the server logs `no tuned FlyDSL config for ('gfx950', 256, M, 5120, 640, 384, 6, ...), using heuristic FlyDSL fallback`.

So the kernel-level route needs three things, not one: relax the geometry and TP gates, add a block-32 -> 1x32 expansion for the shared scales, and tune FHMoE for `(5120, 640, 385, 7)`. The aux-stream route is the cheaper ask.

## 3. GEMM autotuning

Cheapest direction here, and the failure mode is worse than "untuned". On a lookup miss AITER sets `libtype="torch", solidx=0` (`aiter/tuned_gemm.py:215-233`), and `torch_gemm` is plain `F.linear` (`415-451`) - not hipBLASLt, which is a separate `libtype`. A miss is a full drop out of the tuned-kernel path.

We see ~**1,220 such calls per decode step**, all from indexer/compressor shapes:

```
shape is M:128, N:128, K:512 ... not found tuned config in
/tmp/aiter_configs/bf16_tuned_gemm.csv, will use default config! using torch solution:0
```

plus `M:10, N:32320, K:5120` and `N:32, K:5120`. The shipped `aiter/configs/bf16_tuned_gemm.csv` has gfx950 rows, but not for these shapes.

The tooling already exists: `AITER_TUNE_GEMM=1` collects missed shapes into `bf16_untuned_gemm.csv` (`tuned_gemm.py:239-269`), and `aiter/utility/pretune.py` / `base_tuner.py` turn that into tuned rows. Proposal: run the collection over a representative V4.1-Flash decode and prefill sweep, then upstream the resulting gfx950 rows to `aiter/configs/`.

## 4. MoE backend selection and tuning

Two halves of one question: are we on the right MoE backend, and is it tunable?

- In-tree gfx950 configs for this model's expert count **do exist**, but only for the FlyDSL/CK path: `E=384,N=256` and `E=384,N=512` with `device_name=AMD_Instinct_MI355X` / `MI355_OAM`, `dtype=int4_w4a16` (+ `backend=flydsl` variants).
- Our recipe forces `--moe-backend aiter_triton_mxfp4_bf16`, which resolves to `AiterW4A16ExpertsMonolithic` -> Triton `moe_gemm_a16w4`. That path takes **heuristic** kernel configs (`get_kernel_config_triton/gluon`), and AITER ships no gfx950 `a16w4` tuned dispatch tree (only `a8w4/DEFAULT.json`). None of the configs above apply to us.
- Meanwhile the ROCm oracle, under `moe_backend=auto`, explicitly prefers the other one for this routing method: *"DeepSeek-V4 on ROCm: prefer AITER FlyDSL MoE (better perf + accuracy after shuffle/TP-offset fixes), with Triton-unfused as fallback"* (`oracle/mxfp4.py:690-701`).
- And `benchmarks/kernels/benchmark_moe.py` cannot tune our backend even if we wanted to: its dtype choices are `auto`, `fp8_w8a8`, `int8_w8a16`, `int4_w4a16` - no MXFP4 mode (`benchmark_moe.py:1075`).

Proposal, in order: A/B `auto` / `aiter` against the forced `aiter_triton_mxfp4_bf16` on gfx950 and fix the recipe if FlyDSL wins, since tuned configs already exist for it; then add an MXFP4 mode to `benchmark_moe.py` so the Triton `a16w4` path can be tuned at all; then upstream gfx950 `a16w4` configs.

Note #56543 (DNM) proposes the opposite for the same code path -- pinning `backend="triton"` for both AITER W4A16 MoE GEMMs on gfx942/gfx950 -- so the A/B result above is worth reconciling against it before either lands.

## 5. Prefill top-k combine falls back to Torch

`deepseek_v4_1/amd/rocm.py:175-186` uses ordinary Torch indexing because the Triton kernel inherited from V4 "can issue an out-of-bounds access for V4.1's mixed prefill metadata" on gfx950. Prefill-only and small tensors, but it is a known-missing gfx950-safe kernel and TTFT has room.

Addressed by #56638, which re-enables `_combine_topk_swa_indices_kernel` with the three unsafe behaviours fixed in place. It also notes the fallback is not merely slow: `repeat_interleave` on a tensor syncs to the host, so the Torch path cannot be graph-captured at all.

## 6. DSA sparse indexer

Not in the original write-up, and on a newer nightly it is the second-largest block of the decode step after the MoE GEMMs. Splitting it out of the single "indexer/topk" bucket a category summary produces, at 131k context, MoE on the CK a8w4 path:

| | conc 1 | conc 4 | conc 16 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| mqa logits (fp8 paged) | 0.148 | 0.405 | 1.534 |
| candidate mask | 0.048 | 0.133 | 0.464 |
| candidate select | 0.059 | 0.072 | 0.118 |
| topk per row | 0.485 | 0.527 | 0.838 |
| **indexer total** | **0.741** | **1.138** | **2.954** |
| **share of step** | **4.1%** | **6.2%** | **11.4%** |

The composition inverts with concurrency: at 1 the top-k dominates and is launch-bound, at 16 the MQA logits GEMM does. Full detail and method in [this comment](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/56506#issuecomment-5647767775).

### 6.1 Candidate mask sized by the workspace, not the context

`apply_candidate_mask` receives the `[rows, max_model_len]` logits workspace unsliced, so its grid was 1024 programs per row when ~128 have work, and cost tracked rows while ignoring context. Columns at or past a row's `end` are never read: `top_k_per_row_decode` bounds its scan by the same ends, which is also why the no-candidate path skips the mask entirely and reads the same workspace safely.

Addressed by #56628: 2.7x / 4.3x / 6.0x on the kernel at concurrency 1 / 4 / 16, gsm8k 0.9727 / 0.9719.

### 6.2 `next_n` is spent on program count instead of the GEMM tile

The largest single item at concurrency 16 (1.534 ms/step, 5.9% of the step) is `deepgemm_fp8_paged_mqa_logits`. AITER launches `grid = batch * next_n * SplitKV`, so with DSpark 5-token MTP the kernel issues six independent M=32 MFMAs per K block where one M=192 would do.

Its own M-scaling, measured at constant K traffic, is monotone: per unit of work M=64 is 1.27x more efficient than M=32, and M=128 is 1.53x. We sit at the worst point of that curve.
