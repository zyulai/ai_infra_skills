## Overview

On gfx950, DeepSeek-V4.1's fused delayed mHC seams now run as one launch of AITER's HIP Mega-MHC (ROCm/aiter#6520; in AITER since v0.1.27, pinned by #63774, and main now pins v0.1.29, #66596) instead of AITER's two-launch Triton seam (#58655). That covers every seam the Triton kernel serves today, including the gates that #59802 runs on the side stream.

It is a kernel-level saving: 16–41% per seam in the microbenchmark.
- **On the critical path,** profiled serving windows measure about 1% of a C256 decode step and 1.6–1.9% of a 4,096-token prefill step.
- **In serving A/Bs at n = 3 per arm,** decode (C1 to C256) and prefill-heavy alike, the change stays within run-to-run noise. That is what savings of this size predict.

Related:
- **The seam:** #58655 added the Triton seam; #59802 overlaps its gates with attention and FFN; #60153 (draft) adds a gfx942 seam.
- **The kernels:** ROCm/aiter#6520 (HIP Mega-MHC, used here) and ROCm/aiter#6179 (FlyDSL Mega-mHC). ROCm/ATOM#2568 integrates the HIP kernel in ATOM with the same pack-once design.
- **Follow-up:** the seam can also write the MXFP8 input of `wqkv_a` in the same launch; that would build on #59782 and #57857.
- **Other frameworks:** SGLang's AMD DeepSeek-V4.1 path runs its prefill and DSpark draft seams through AITER's FlyDSL Mega-mHC, behind opt-in flags (sgl-project/sglang#49283 and sgl-project/sglang#49284, open).

See Details.

## Claims

- **One launch per seam instead of two** for every fused seam on gfx950: two per decoder layer, plus the DSpark draft's.
  - **Per seam** (MI350X, this PR's benchmark, layer-30 weights, HIP-graph replay over cold inputs and weights): HIP Mega-MHC is 16–34% faster than main's Triton seam with the post block and 27–41% without. With the post block that is 2.7–5.3 µs per seam at T 1–256.
  - **In serving,** it replaces every Triton seam call, on the main stream and on #59802's side stream: the C128, C256 and prefill traces show no Triton seam kernel on any queue or rank. In those traces, the median seam takes 26–29% less time than the two Triton kernels at decode sizes, and 35% less at 4,096 tokens (54.3 against 83.5 µs).
- **Where the seam is on the critical path, it saves step time,** measured per kernel in profiled serving windows (all 8 ranks):
  - **C256 decode:** steps carry 192 tokens per rank, above the overlap's 128-token limit, so every seam runs serially with nothing else running. Mega-MHC cuts the seam from 2,069 to 1,541 µs per step (−528 µs, −25.5%), about 1.0% of the ~50.75 ms step.
  - **Prefill** (random 8192/256 at C32): prefill and mixed steps never take the overlap path, so all 86 seams of a 4,096-token step run serially and 100% exposed.
    - Mega-MHC cuts the seam from 7.1–7.5 ms to 4.6–4.7 ms per step: −2.5 to −2.8 ms.
    - That is 1.6–1.9% of the 140–177 ms step, or 0.60–0.68 ms per 1,000 prefill tokens.
  - **Elsewhere:** the seam is serial the same way in eager and PIECEWISE modes and in the draft.
- **Serving: no gain beyond noise at n = 3 per arm** (DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash, DP8 + EP8, MI350X, 0 failed requests).
  - **Decode** (random 1024/1024; Nov 6, before the rebase):
    - **C128:** decode takes #59802's overlap, which keeps its post-collapse kernel on the critical path and runs the gates on the side stream, so this PR mostly shortens hidden work. The acceptance-normalized step time is unchanged (−0.02%, p 0.92). Mean ITL, which counts one streamed chunk per step and so measures the step directly, moved −0.02% too. TPOT p50's +1.5% follows the 0.7% lower DSpark acceptance length, and it sits inside the main arm's own spread (14.51–15.10 ms over its three runs). With speculative decoding off (this head, Nov 11), where TPOT is the step time, C128 TPOT p50 moves −0.05% and ITL p50 +0.01%.
    - **C256:** the acceptance-normalized step time moved −1.0% (p 0.05). That matches the trace's ~1.0%, but it sits at the noise bar.
  - **Low-concurrency decode** (this head, Nov 10; random 1024/1024 at C1, C4, C16 and C32): no metric regresses beyond noise.
    - The acceptance-normalized step time moves +0.04%, −0.07%, −0.12% and −0.29%, inside bars of ±0.5–1.1%. ITL p50 moves −0.01% to −0.31%.
    - At these sizes every decode step takes #59802's overlap. A profiled C4 window shows its post-collapse kernel unchanged on the critical path, with Mega-MHC replacing only the side-stream gates.
  - **Prefill-heavy** (this head; random 8192/256 at C32 and C64, and 4096/256 at C64):
    - **8k:** flat. TTFT p50 and input throughput change by under 3%, with p ≥ 0.26.
    - **4k at C64:** input throughput +1.45% (p 0.026) and TPOT p50 −2.7% (p 0.061). The throughput change clears twice the same-arm sd (±1.1%), but not twice the same-arm range (±2.0%).
  - **Consistent with the traces:**
    - The measured prefill saving predicts ~2% on TTFT and ~1% on input throughput for this load. That is inside the 8k configs' two-sd bars (±5.7–9.8% TTFT, ±5.9–6.4% throughput), and right at 4k/C64's ±1.1%, where +1.45% was measured.
    - The C256 decode saving predicts ~1%, where −1.0% was measured at the bar.
    - n = 3 serving on this node doesn't resolve changes of this size.
- **Numerics:** not bitwise against the Triton seam. Both do the same fp32 math with the same bf16 hi/lo split of `fn`, summed in another order.
  - **The unit test:** the residual stays within one bf16 ulp of a float64 reference, plus the fp32 summation bound. Every other output is within twice the Triton seam's relative error, plus a small floor. It passes at every tested T on MI350X and MI355X.
  - **Outside the test's T set:** on its random weights with the post block, the benchmark measures `post_mix` at 4 tokens at 3.0e-5 relative error for both HIP and FlyDSL, against 1.0e-6 for Triton.
    - The unit test passes with T 4 added (MI350X) and with T 3–8 added (MI355X). With the test's inputs, HIP's `post_mix` error at T 4 is 5.7e-6 against Triton's 5.9e-6 (MI355X).
    - So the case is specific to the benchmark's inputs; no unit test reproduces it.
  - **GSM8K** (5-shot, greedy, 1,319 questions, strict-match): 0.9227 ± 0.0074 on main and 0.9287 ± 0.0071 with this head (Nov 9). Before the rebase, the Nov 6 run gave 0.9272 ± 0.0072 and 0.9204 ± 0.0075. Both runs are within the combined stderr.
  - **GPQA Diamond** (this head, Nov 10; thinking on at `reasoning_effort` high, temperature 0.6, top_p 0.95, 8 samples a question): 89.08 ± 1.35% on main and 88.64 ± 0.76% with this head (mean ± sd over 8 runs of 198; ±1.9 stderr across questions). Paired by question, the difference is −0.44 ± 0.58 points (p 0.44); the design detects about 1.6 points.
- **Weight reloads:** `fn` is packed once after loading. Every reload and in-place update reruns `process_weights_after_loading` (#65914), which refills the packs in place with `refill_derived` (#64692), so CUDA graphs captured earlier read the new weights.
- **A model-load bug** that the MI350X validation found (the server failed to start under the loader's bf16 default dtype) is fixed, with a regression test; see Validation.
- **Scope:** no new env var or config field.
  - **Where the Triton seam stays:** off gfx950, with `VLLM_ROCM_USE_AITER` unset or 0 (its default), and with DBO.
  - **No fallback for an old AITER:** the probe relies on the pinned AITER shipping `aiter.ops.mega_mhc`, as the Triton seam's probe relies on it for its own kernel. An AITER older than v0.1.27 now fails at load with `ModuleNotFoundError` rather than keeping the Triton seam. `VLLM_ROCM_USE_AITER=0` avoids that, but turns off the other AITER paths too.
  - **#59802's split is unchanged:** its post-collapse kernel stays on the critical path, and only its side-stream gates switch kernels.

+def _mhc_fused_seam_fp64(
+    residual,
+    fn,
+    hc_scale,
+    hc_base,
+    rms_eps,
+    hc_pre_eps,
+    hc_sinkhorn_eps,
+    hc_post_mult_value,
+    sinkhorn_repeat,
+    pre_mix=None,
+    norm_weight=None,
+    norm_eps=1e-6,
+    sublayer_out=None,
+    post_layer_mix=None,
+    comb_res_mix=None,

+    """The fused delayed seam in float64, rounded to bf16 where the kernels
+    round: the new residual, the collapse and the normed layer input."""
+    f64, n = torch.float64, residual.shape[1]
+    if sublayer_out is not None:
+        residual = (
+            torch.einsum("tij,tih->tjh", comb_res_mix.to(f64), residual.to(f64))
+            + post_layer_mix.to(f64) * sublayer_out.to(f64).unsqueeze(1)
+        ).to(torch.bfloat16)
+    x = residual.flatten(1).to(f64)

+    scale, base = hc_scale.to(f64), hc_base.to(f64)
+    pre = torch.sigmoid(mixes[:, :n] * scale[0] + base[:n]) + hc_pre_eps
+    post = (
+        torch.sigmoid(mixes[:, n : 2 * n] * scale[1] + base[n : 2 * n])
+        * hc_post_mult_value
+    )
+    comb = mixes[:, 2 * n :] * scale[2] + base[2 * n :]
+    comb = sinkhorn_normalize_ref(comb.view(-1, n, n), sinkhorn_repeat, hc_sinkhorn_eps)
+    collapse = (
+        residual[:, 0]
+        if pre_mix is None
+        else (pre_mix.to(f64).unsqueeze(-1) * residual.to(f64)).sum(1)
+    ).to(torch.bfloat16)
+    layer_input = F.rms_norm(
+        collapse.to(f64), collapse.shape[-1:], norm_weight.to(f64), norm_eps
+    ).to(torch.bfloat16)
+    return residual, post.unsqueeze(-1), comb, layer_input, pre
