## Overview

Use the native `merge_attn_states` kernel on CDNA3 and newer (gfx942, gfx950, gfx1250) instead of the Triton fallback. The kernel is already built and registered on ROCm. The Python dispatch in `vllm/v1/attention/ops/merge_attn_states.py` is gated on `get_cdna_version() >= 3`, so gfx90a stays on Triton. The kernel was introduced for CUDA in #16173.

## Claims

- On gfx942 and gfx950, the native kernel is faster than the Triton kernel in every case of `tests/kernels/attention/test_merge_attn_states.py`: 2592 timed cases per GPU, median 2.4x, minimum 1.1x.
- The native output matches Triton on both architectures, including FP8 output (`float8_e4m3fnuz` on gfx942, `float8_e4m3fn` on gfx950).
- In end-to-end serving of an MLA model, the merge kernel time drops about 4x. Serving metrics are unchanged within run-to-run noise, because merge is a small share of prefill.
- gfx90a (CDNA2) and RDNA stay on Triton. `get_cdna_version() >= 3` also selects gfx1250 (CDNA5); that GPU was not part of the runs below.

## Validation

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| MI325X | gfx942 | 2598 passed | min 1.10x, median 2.39x, max 8.58x, 0 slower |
| MI355X | gfx950 | 2598 passed | min 1.64x, median 2.36x, max 8.30x, 0 slower |

### End-to-end

Kimi-K3, 8x MI355X, TP=8, FP8 KV cache, `--load-format dummy`, concurrency 16. Each configuration was run once without and once with this change; nothing else differs between the two runs. All numbers are from the profiled runs. Merge launches and merge kernel time come from the rank-0 torch profiler trace over the same window of steps, which covers one full request. TTFT, TPOT and throughput are from the same runs, with the profiler active for that window.

| input/output | merge kernel | merge launches | merge kernel time | mean TTFT (ms) | median TTFT (ms) | mean TPOT (ms) | output tok/s |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 60k/600 | Triton | 1176 | 118.90 ms | 7280.80 | 3337.37 | 89.97 | 152.49 |
| 60k/600 | native | 1176 | 29.30 ms | 7291.12 | 3231.90 | 89.54 | 153.09 |
| 128k/1k | Triton | 3744 | 424.50 ms | 17272.42 | 9884.53 | 111.25 | 122.19 |
| 128k/1k | native | 3792 | 98.31 ms | 17312.48 | 10211.05 | 110.82 | 122.54 |

The merge kernel is 4.1x faster at 60k and 4.3x faster at 128k. Over the profiled window it is 0.26% (60k) and 0.42% (128k) of total kernel time under Triton, so the serving metrics move within noise.

### Qwen2.5-7B on one MI350X

gfx950, nightly `vllm/vllm-openai-rocm:nightly-rocm100-36768d1bf` (`0.30.1rc1.dev312+g36768d1bf`). Both arms use `--attention-backend ROCM_AITER_FA`, prefix caching off, and eager mode. The only difference is the dispatch file: stock `is_cuda()` (Triton on ROCm) versus `get_cdna_version() >= 3` (native).

#### Accuracy

`lm_eval` task `niah_single_2`, 500 samples at each length, seed 1234, `max_seq_lengths` 8192, 16384, and 32768. The same command was run at `max_num_batched_tokens` 8192 and 1024. 16k and 32k prompts chunk at both budgets. 8k prompts chunk at 1024 and stay in one chunk at 8192.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 8192 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 |
| 16384 | 0.998 | 0.998 | 0.998 | 0.998 |
| 32768 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 |

The 16k score is the same single miss on all four runs.
