## Summary

In `load_moe_expert_weights`, a sliced/transposed fused MoE expert weight view is passed to `weight_loader()` and copied to GPU while still non-contiguous. A non-contiguous CPU tensor's DMA transfer to GPU is dramatically slower than a contiguous one. Adds `.contiguous()` right before the `weight_loader()` call, on the already-sliced piece — not upfront on the whole weight, since a prior attempt at this fix (#32081, auto-closed by staleness, not rejected) found that naive blanket `.contiguous()` can regress smaller tensors.

## Scope note

A synthetic benchmark on H100 NVL (the real Llama-4 checkpoints, 109B-400B, don't fit on one GPU) tested three locations that could have the same issue. Only the fused MoE expert weight path (this PR) showed an unambiguous, large improvement — FP8 block-scale and Q/K rotary permute tensors were *slower* with `.contiguous()` added at their (smaller) sizes in isolation. Since MoE expert weights dominate total checkpoint bytes, this is the change with validated, measured benefit; the other two locations are deliberately excluded pending more precise real-world sizing data.
