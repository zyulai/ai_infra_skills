## PR body
```markdown
On gfx11 (RDNA3/3.5) a W4A16 GEMM loses a large share of its achievable read bandwidth when an operand's row stride lands on a multiple of 1024 bytes. The rows alias into the same L2/DDR channels and the accesses serialize. Offsetting the stride by one 128 B cache line dodges it.

This PR nudges **both** operands off that cliff: the packed weight row (padded once at load) and the activation row (padded per call) and improves TTFT by up to 23 % and TPOT by up to 3 %.
Padding the activation (at runtime) pays for itself due to reduced GEMM time.

## The two cliffs

For weights, we pad at load time when they are a multiple of 1024 bytes. For activations,
we only pad when they are a multiple of 2048 bytes to ensure that their cost is recovered by the GEMM wins.

The following table shows how many GEMM shapes are affected by the padding in a model:

| Model | weight padded | 
|---|---|---|
| `Qwen3-8B-quantized.w4a16` | 4/4 (`K=4096` → 2048 B, `K=12288` → 6144 B) | 
| `Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct-w4a16` | 4/4 (`K=14336` → 7168 B qualifies) | 
| `gemma-3-4b-it-quantized.w4a16` | 2/3 (`K=2048`, `K=10240`) |
| `Qwen3-4B-quantized.w4a16` | 1/4 (`o_proj`, `K=4096`) |

Gated on gfx1151, where the effect was measured. This might also apply to other gfx11 parts, but we will need to measure and can enable as a follow up.

## Implementation

- `pack_skinny_int4` is the single source of truth for the skinny weight layout and applies the weight pad once at load.
- Activations are padded by an explicit copy inside the W4A16 apply op. The copy costs less than the GEMM gains by the strides. Having the activation's producer emit a padded output would make it free; that is a reasonable follow-up.
- **Activations are not padded on the decode path.** . This is why the decode win is preserved intact (see TPOT below).
```
