## PR body
```markdown
## Purpose

Avoid the odd-row performance cliff in per-token-group 8-bit quantization while preserving the existing launch policy for even row counts.

We identified this while investigating unexpectedly poor TPOT (time per output token) with Gemma 4 31B FP8 at TP8 on H100. The reproduction traced about 35 ms of extra time in a 13,359-token prefill forward pass to activation quantization, enough to erase the roughly 30 ms saved by FP8 GEMMs. At TP8, the down-projection input has width 2688: odd row counts launch 16-thread blocks and make quantization roughly 4× slower than the neighboring even case. The width-5376 inputs also take a roughly 2× slower path.

The current heuristic selects one group per block when the total group count is odd, launching only 16 threads. This change selects block geometry using the next even row count, then uses ceil-div and bounds checks for the actual groups. No tensors are padded or copied. All threads still participate in block and PDL synchronization, and the bounds check is compiled out for full blocks.

Quantization arithmetic and scale layouts are unchanged. The helper serves both FP8 and INT8, so both receive regression coverage. The separate packed kernel is unchanged.

### Related work

[#55330](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/pull/55330) takes a different approach: a register-resident kernel with fixed 256-thread launches for eligible inputs. This change retains the shared-memory kernel and its existing even-row policy, including support for FP32 inputs and group size 64. The approaches may be complementary, with this policy improving the shared-memory fallback.

## Performance

The H100 measurements below confirm the kernel improvement at the Gemma TP8 down-projection shape. End-to-end TP8 TPOT has not yet been remeasured with this patch.

Kernel latency in microseconds, measured with CUPTI and CUDA graphs. BF16 input, FP8 output, group size 128, column-major FP32 scales with UE8M0 rounding. Results are medians of three rounds of 30 samples; cold runs flush L2 outside the timed kernel.

| GPU | M × K | Hot before → after | Cold before → after |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| H100 | 32767 × 128 | 41.76 → 10.45 | 41.86 → 12.48 |
| H100 | 32768 × 128 | 10.46 → 10.43 | 12.35 → 12.35 |
| H100 | 65537 × 128 | 81.18 → 41.82 | 81.22 → 41.86 |
| H100 | 13359 × 2688 | 339.63 → 81.46 | 339.63 → 83.38 |
| GB10 | 32767 × 128 | 49.23 → 20.93 | 85.98 → 89.10 |
| GB10 | 32768 × 128 | 20.80 → 20.82 | 87.14 → 86.62 |
| GB10 | 65537 × 128 | 108.24 → 67.47 | 146.27 → 145.66 |
| GB10 | 13359 × 2688 | 517.30 → 487.12 | 541.51 → 516.96 |

**H100:** The final production-source comparison covers 18 shapes, with geometric-mean latency reductions of 39.5% hot and 38.0% cold. An earlier 259-shape policy prototype also covered the broader matrix: all 112 odd shapes were within 2.9% hot and 4.2% cold of the next-even control. That prototype always enabled the guard for odd rows; the final patch omits it when no tail exists.

**GB10:** Across 182 general shapes, the geometric-mean latency reduction is 6.7% hot and 0.2% cold. Even-row performance remains essentially unchanged. Across 91 odd/next-even pairs, geometric-mean latency differs by less than 0.4%.

Clocks were not locked on either GPU. The H100 and GB10 aggregate matrices differ, so their averages are not a direct GPU comparison. H200, B200, B300, and ROCm were not tested.
