## Motivation

This is a ROCm performance change: it routes Qwen3.8's 512-expert softmax MoE router through aiter's `topk_gating` instead of the vendored `vllm::moe::topkGatingSoftmax` launcher it reaches today. Against its merge-base on the aiter pin that SGLang ships (`285579b62`, since #45498, with [Rocm/aiter5334](https://github.com/ROCm/aiter/pull/5334)), it gives **+1.27%** output tok/s without MTP on 8x MI355X, TP8, geomean over 12 serving shapes. On v0.5.19 with NEXTN MTP it gave **+2.22%**.

On SGLang v0.5.20 at the default page size the gain is +1.03% without MTP and +0.40% with MTP; see https://github.com/sgl-project/sglang/pull/40708#issuecomment-5933064541. 

Qwen3.8 picks 10 experts out of 512 on every one of its 92 layers. On ROCm that pick is 0.878 ms per decode step, 4.8% of decode kernel time, against 0.440 ms/step on B300. `aiter.fused_moe.fused_topk` only hands off to its ASM router for `(experts, topk)` in `(128,4), (128,6), (128,8), (256,6), (256,8), (384,8), (640,8)`, and Qwen3.8 is `(512, 10)`. Qwen3.8-Flash-Next has the same `(512, 10)` softmax router at hidden size 2560 and takes the same path.

[#28399](https://github.com/sgl-project/sglang/pull/28399) tried the same wiring in August and got nothing for it (ITL +3.7%, throughput -1.9% on Qwen3.5 TP2). aiter has since gained the E=512 kernel and learned to honour `need_renorm` for softmax ([Rocm/aiter4460](https://github.com/ROCm/aiter/pull/4460)).

## Modifications

- `python/sglang/srt/layers/moe/topk.py`: `_use_aiter_topk_gating_softmax()` decides, and the `_use_aiter` softmax branch calls `topk_gating` when it says yes. Every other shape keeps `aiter_fused_topk`.
- `python/sglang/srt/environ.py`: `SGLANG_ROCM_DISABLE_AITER_TOPK_GATING`, default off. Set it to 1 to fall back to `fused_topk`.
- `test/registered/amd/test_qwen38_moe_softmax_topk_gating.py`: agreement with the legacy launcher at 4, 128, 1024, 1025, 2048 and 4096 rows at hidden sizes 8192 and 2560, so both aiter kernels are covered up to the ceiling, plus every envelope boundary.

The dispatch: gfx95, AITER on, hidden size 8192 (Qwen3.8) or 2560 (Qwen3.8-Flash-Next), BF16 hidden states, BF16 contiguous gating output, 512 experts, at most 4096 rows, no correction bias, no fused shared slots, no packed output.

## Speed Tests and Profiling

8x MI355X, TP8, Qwen3.8-2.4T-A95B-Quark-MXFP4, `chunked-prefill-size 16384, page-size 64`. 12 cells per arm: 8k/1k at concurrency 4 through 256, 60k/600 at 4, 16 and 64, 128k/1k at 4 and 16. Geomeans of output tok/s. 

The four arms ran on the same node and differ only in which kernel the softmax router call reaches:

- **baseline**: v0.5.19 as released. Every router call goes through `aiter_fused_topk`, which for `(512, 10)` lands on the vendored `topkGatingSoftmax`.
- **aiter `topk_gating`, no ceiling**: this PR without the 4096-row limit. Every router call, including the 16384-token prefill chunks, goes to `topk_gating`. Above 1024 rows `topk_gating` switches from the E=512 kernel to a shared-memory kernel, which loses to `topkGatingSoftmax` above 4096 rows (see the microbenchmark below).
- **this PR**: `topk_gating` up to 4096 rows, which covers decode, verify, draft-extend and short prefill chunks. Larger calls stay on `topkGatingSoftmax`.
- **#39986 Triton router, ceiling raised to 1024**: [#39986](https://github.com/sgl-project/sglang/pull/39986) sends 512-expert softmax routing to a Triton kernel, but only at hidden size 4096 (Qwen3.5) and at most 128 rows, so on main it never fires for Qwen3.8. For this arm its gate was widened to hidden size 8192 and at most 1024 rows, enough for every decode and verify step in this sweep. Larger calls stay on `topkGatingSoftmax`.

Graph times approximate kernel GPU time; eager times include launch overhead. On GPU time `topk_gating` leads through 1024 rows, is within 0.3 us of legacy at 2048 and 4096, and falls clearly behind from 5120 (12.92 vs 11.51 us, 28.58 vs 18.56 at 16384). Eager it stays ahead through 6144. The ceiling is 4096, the largest size where it is within noise on GPU time and still ahead eager.

Why `topk_gating` rather than extending #39986 to Qwen3.8. Under CUDA graphs the Triton kernel is the fastest of the three, 0.5-0.75 us per call up to 1024 rows. Decode, verify and draft replay from graphs, so that lead is real, but it is small next to a decode step: in the TP0 traces Triton saves 9-20 us per graph replay of 7.7-14.2 ms, 0.1-0.15% of GPU time, which is why the end-to-end numbers above tie.

Where they differ is eager. Triton is the slowest of the three there (about 21 us per call, against 12 us for `topk_gating` and 15 us for `topkGatingSoftmax`), but those times are mostly launch overhead, so this matters only for prefills short enough to be launch-bound, which this sweep did not measure.

The two PRs don't conflict. #39986 fires only at hidden size 4096 (Qwen3.5) and this PR only at 8192 (Qwen3.8) and 2560 (Qwen3.8-Flash-Next). 

Without [Rocm/aiter5334](https://github.com/ROCm/aiter/pull/5334), `topk_gating` falls back to a generic kernel at 8.0-9.2 us in graphs through 1024 rows, barely ahead of legacy.

Qwen3.8-Flash-Next, measured by @amd-nprotaso in review: output tok/s +2.17%, median TPOT 7.71 -> 7.54 ms (-2.25%). Accept length came out 0.7% higher on the patch side, so per decode step the gain is -1.4% (17.84 -> 17.59 ms). 

Not measured: other 512-expert models, which keep their current dispatch; short-prompt prefill (chunks <= 4096 rows) end to end; gfx942; anything outside TP8/EP1.
